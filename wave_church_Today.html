I

Introduction

Psalm 50:4–5

4God calls to the heavens above and the earth below to judge His people. 5He says, Gather My faithful people before Me, those who have made a covenant with Me through sacrifice.

God came as Judge — and as Teacher
Last time, through Asaph’s Psalm 50, we reflected together on the message proclaimed to the people of Israel who had gathered to remember their covenant with God and commit themselves to standing before Him once again. Although God came as Judge, He wanted to teach the people of Israel who He is.
Ps. 50:4–5
Where the command came from
Today, we will look at where the command for the Israelites to remember this covenant every seven years and renew their relationship with God before Him came from. We will also consider how this command, which Moses left for the generations after him, can apply to us today.
Deut. 31:10–12
II

Do Not Forget God’s Word

Joshua 8:33

33All Israel, including their elders, officers, and judges, both native-born Israelites and foreigners, stood on both sides of the ark before the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the LORD’s covenant. Half stood in front of Mount Gerizim, and half stood in front of Mount Ebal, just as Moses, the servant of the LORD, had previously commanded for the blessing of the people of Israel.

Gathered between the two mountains
After entering the land of Canaan under Joshua’s leadership, the Israelites won the battle at Jericho. They suffered defeat at Ai but soon won the next battle there. Afterward, they gathered between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim to remember their covenant with God and remind the nation of Israel of the words of blessing and cursing.
Josh. 8:30–33
A command left behind by Moses
Interestingly, this was something Moses had commanded in his farewell message before the Israelites crossed the Jordan River into the land of Canaan. Moses taught the Israelites about the blessings and curses connected to their relationship with God, and he wanted them to remember these things even after entering Canaan.
Deut. 11:29–32
Joshua 8:34–35

34Afterward, Joshua read all the words of the law, the blessings and the curses, according to everything written in the Book of the Law. 35There was not a single word of all that Moses had commanded that Joshua failed to read before the whole assembly of Israel, including the women, children, and foreigners living among them.

Listening together to the whole law
At this time, half of the people stood in front of Mount Gerizim, and the other half stood in front of Mount Ebal. As they listened to Joshua read all the words of the law, they remembered their covenant with the God who had led them this far and took this time to commit themselves to living as His covenant people.
Josh. 8:34–35
No one left out
The whole assembly heard the Word — women, children, and the foreigners living among them. Remembering the covenant was never the task of leaders alone; it belonged to every person who stood before the LORD that day.
Josh. 8:35 · Deut. 31:12
Deuteronomy 11:29–32

29When the LORD your God brings you into the land you are going to possess, proclaim the blessing on Mount Gerizim and the curse on Mount Ebal. … 32Be careful to follow all the statutes and laws that I am setting before you today.

Blessing and curse
Moses taught the Israelites about the blessings and curses connected to their relationship with God, and he wanted them to remember these things even after entering Canaan — which is exactly what Joshua carried out between the two mountains.
Deut. 11:29–32 · Josh. 8:33
Careful obedience
The land was a gift, but life in the land was to be shaped by the Word. Moses’ concern was not the crossing of the Jordan but what the people would live by once they were on the other side.
Deut. 11:32
Deuteronomy 27:4–8

4When you cross the Jordan, set up these stones on Mount Ebal, as I command you today, and coat them with lime. … 6Build an altar to the LORD your God with uncut stones, and offer burnt offerings on it to the LORD your God. 7Offer peace offerings as well, eat there, and rejoice before the LORD your God. 8Write all the words of this law clearly and accurately on those stones.

An altar on Mount Ebal
Moses commanded the Israelites to build an altar on Mount Ebal and offer burnt offerings and peace offerings to God — worship was to be the first thing established in the new land.
Deut. 27:4–6
Enjoying His presence
They were to eat there and rejoice before the LORD their God — to enjoy being in His presence, not merely to complete a ritual.
Deut. 27:7
So they would not forget
He also commanded them to write the words of this law clearly on stones so that they would not forget them. We can see his deep desire for them to live according to God’s Word even after entering the land of Canaan.
Deut. 27:8
Genesis 12:6–7

6Abram traveled through the land as far as the oak of Moreh at Shechem. At that time, the Canaanites were living in the land. 7The LORD appeared to Abram and said, I will give this land to your descendants. So Abram built an altar there to the LORD, who had appeared to him.

Shechem — the first place of worship
Shechem, where Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim are located, became the first place where the Israelites worshiped after entering the land of Canaan. Until David later made Jerusalem the center of worship and government, this was a very important place for the Israelites in the Promised Land.
2 Sam. 5:6–9 · 2 Sam. 6:12–17
The promise given at Shechem
Shechem was also where God promised Abraham that He would give this land to his descendants. Generations later, his descendants stood in that same place to hear the law read aloud.
Gen. 12:6–7
John 4:20–21

20Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, but you say that Jerusalem is the place where people should worship. 21Jesus said to her, “Woman, believe Me, the hour is coming when you will neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, worship the Father.

Mount Gerizim and the Samaritans
Shechem was such an important place that the Samaritans later made Mount Gerizim their place of worship. For the Israelites, it was part of the land God had promised, and it was also the location of Jacob’s well.
John 4:5–6 · 4:20
Where Jesus met the Samaritan woman
This was the very place where Jesus met and spoke with the Samaritan woman. The mountain that had held Israel’s memory of the covenant became the place where Jesus pointed beyond every mountain to the Father Himself.
John 4:20–21
III

The Blessed Life of Living by God’s Word

Deuteronomy 31:10–12 · Main Text

10Moses commanded them, At the end of every seven years, during the Feast of Tabernacles in the year of release, 11when all Israel gathers before the LORD your God at the place He chooses, read this law aloud so that all Israel can hear it. 12… so that they may fear the LORD your God and carefully obey all the words of this law.

Every seven years
Moses commanded the Israelites to read the words of this law every seven years at the place God would choose, which would later be Jerusalem, so that they would obey them.
Deut. 31:10–11
Read aloud for all to hear
The whole nation was to hear the law read together. This is the command behind the gathering Asaph described in Psalm 50 — a people assembled before God to remember whose they are.
Deut. 31:11 · Ps. 50:5
Why Moses gave this command
Moses did not want the Israelites to follow the pagan customs of Canaan and abandon the LORD their God, giving up His blessings and bringing curses upon themselves.
Deut. 31:12
Romans 12:2

2Do not follow the pattern of this age, but be transformed through the renewing of your mind, so that you may discern God’s good, pleasing, and perfect will.

Does this apply to us?
This call to live by God’s Word also applies to us today. Of course, the curses and blessings spoken to Israel belonged to the covenant between God and Israel, the two parties to that covenant, and do not apply to us as Gentiles.
Rom. 12:2
Not swept along
However, as Christians, being swept along by the world’s customs and ways of thinking is not what God wants for His children.
Rom. 12:2
Psalm 1:1–3

1Blessed is the person who does not follow the advice of the wicked, stand in the way of sinners, or sit among those who mock, 2but delights in the LORD’s law and meditates on it day and night. 3That person is like a tree planted beside streams of water, bearing fruit in season, with leaves that do not wither. Everything that person does prospers.

How the Psalms begin
The Book of Psalms, with its 150 psalms, begins by declaring the blessedness of living according to God’s Word.
Ps. 1:1–2
A tree by the water
The blessed life is not a life without seasons, but a life rooted where the water never runs out — fruit in season, leaves that do not wither.
Ps. 1:3 · Jer. 17:7–8
Psalm 119:9

9How can a young person keep his conduct pure? By following Your word.

The only way
The writer of Psalm 119 also sings that the only way for a young person to live rightly is to follow God’s Word.
Ps. 119:9
Psalm 73:3, 17, 28

3For I envied the arrogant when I saw the wicked prosper. … 17Only when I entered God’s sanctuary did I understand their final outcome. … 28But for me, being near God is good. …

Asaph’s trouble
In Psalm 73, Asaph is troubled when he sees the wicked prospering even though they live without God’s Word.
Ps. 73:3
Seeing the end
His question was not answered by better arguments but by entering God’s sanctuary, where he finally understood their final outcome.
Ps. 73:16–17
Nearness as blessing
After understanding their final outcome, he declares that being near God is his blessing.
Ps. 73:28
IV

Putting a Life of Living by the Word into Practice

Matthew 4:4

4Jesus answered, It is written that people do not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.

#1 — Read Scripture every day
We can put a life of living by the Word into practice by reading Scripture every day.
Ps. 1:1–3 · Josh. 1:8 · Matt. 4:4
Proverbs 3:5–6

5Trust the LORD with your whole heart, and do not depend on your own understanding. 6Acknowledge Him in everything you do, and He will guide your way.

#2 — Trust God’s Word
We can put a life of living by the Word into practice by trusting God’s Word.
Prov. 3:5–6 · Matt. 6:30–32 · Matt. 7:24–27
In everything you do
Trusting the Word is not a feeling but a daily practice — acknowledging Him in the ordinary decisions where our own understanding feels sufficient.
Prov. 3:6
Philippians 3:8–9

8I consider everything a loss because knowing Christ Jesus my Lord is of far greater worth. For His sake I have lost everything, and I consider it all rubbish so that I may gain Christ 9and be found in Him. …

#3 — Know Jesus, who is the Word
We can put a life of living by the Word into practice by desiring to know Jesus, who is the Word, more deeply.
John 1:1, 14 · John 5:39–40 · Phil. 3:8–9
Gaining Christ
Paul measures everything he once counted as gain against one thing: being found in Christ. A life shaped by the Word ends here, in a Person.
Phil. 3:8–9
